# Golden Circle messaging

Connect an organization’s purpose to the actions that express it and the actual offer. Use the framework as a communication tool, not proof of product demand, conversion, loyalty, or scientific claims about the brain.

## Establish the circle

- **Why:** the purpose, cause, or belief. Profit is an outcome, not the purpose. Distinguish the organization’s belief from a customer benefit and from verified customer motivation. Mark a newly proposed belief as a hypothesis.
- **How:** the distinctive actions, principles, or product behaviors that enact the belief. Explain why each supports the purpose. Generic claims such as quality, innovation, or caring need concrete evidence.
- **What:** the product, service, or specific action being offered. Make it understandable without decoding the philosophy.

Identify the audience, their occasion or problem, the finished result, and a plausible reason to act or pay. Use available customer feedback and actual product behavior. Do not invent testimonials, outcomes, functionality, or audience demand. Preserve the user’s chosen product and scope.

## Apply it to communication

Build the message from purpose toward delivery, but do not rigidly force every artifact into three labeled sections. On a website, show a clear product description and useful demonstration near the emotional headline. Visitors should understand what they can do immediately.

For promotional UI, connect:

1. A meaningful promise and a concrete product description.
2. Visible proof using real product examples, accurately labeled concepts, or actual demonstrations.
3. The user’s steps and the finished result, including distinct creator and visitor roles when relevant.
4. The offer, price, and material restrictions next to the claims they qualify.
5. A clear next action appropriate to the visitor’s intent.

Use a coherent visual direction drawn from the product’s subject. Prefer supporting details that explain the offer over decoration. Do not change the core product, pricing, policies, or code merely to make a proposed message true. Flag any promise that requires a separate product decision.

Check the **How** against access restrictions, free/paid differences, privacy, cancellation, ordering, sharing, and other material terms relevant to the claim. Scope these checks to the product under discussion. Never obscure restrictions in emotional language.

## Deliver the requested artifact

For an assessment or proposal, provide:

- A concise Why/How/What mapping.
- Specific changes to the current message or layout and the reason for each.
- Example copy that pairs emotional meaning with product clarity.
- A short list of product/claim mismatches, if any.
- A practical validation approach using comprehension and meaningful actions, not clicks alone.

For implementation, respect authorized scope and preserve unrelated behavior. For suggestions-only requests, do not edit, deploy, or create files unless separately requested.

Avoid treating people who reject the experience as confused. Test whether they understand the offer, have a relevant occasion, complete the intended task, and have a reason to return or pay. Purpose-led and concrete outcome-led headlines are both reasonable candidates to compare; do not promise a winner in advance.

## Sources and boundaries

Primary definition: https://simonsinek.com/golden-circle/

Homepage clarity and immediate product understanding: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/

Sinek’s claims about inspiration are the author’s framework, not universal causal findings. Do not repeat simplified limbic-brain explanations as settled science. When research is requested, follow claims to primary sources, identify unavailable sources, and distinguish the framework’s assertions from independent evidence.
